## 1) Scientific accounts (compressed, contested)

### 1.1 Affect (broad)
In affective science, **affect** often means a primitive evaluative tone of experience — roughly “how things are going for the organism right now” — often decomposed into:
- **Valence** — pleasant ↔ unpleasant  
- **Arousal** — activated ↔ deactivated  

These are **constructs** inferred from self-report, behavior, physiology, and neural correlates — not meters that read “feeling” directly.

### 1.2 Interoception
**Interoception** = sensing the body’s internal state (heartbeat, gut, breath, inflammation, etc.). Many theories treat bodily signals as a major ingredient of feeling. Measured via: heartbeat detection tasks, peripheral physiology (HR, GSR, cortisol), imaging of insula/ACC (correlates, not proof of experience).

### 1.3 Basic-emotion theories
Claim: a small set of discrete emotions (e.g. fear, anger, disgust, sadness, happiness…) are biologically privileged packages (expression, physiology, action tendency).  
**Support:** some cross-cultural recognition patterns; some conserved defensive behaviors.  
**Falsifiers / limits:** high variability of expression; context dependence; weak one-to-one physiology fingerprints for each label.

### 1.4 Constructionist / constructed-emotion theories
Claim: emotions are **constructed** from more basic ingredients (affect, concepts, language, situation) rather than fixed biological “keys.”  
**Support:** same physiology can map to different emotion labels; language/culture shapes reports; category variability.  
**Falsifiers / limits:** must still explain fast, stereotyped defensive reactions and developmental regularities.

### 1.5 Prediction-error / allostasis (careful link)
Under predictive-processing / allostasis views: the brain continuously predicts bodily and world states and updates on **prediction error**; affect may index expected/realized success at regulating the body toward viable set-points (energy, temperature, safety…).  
**Useful for engineering analogies** (error signals, regulation).  
**Not licensed:** “we run a prediction-error loop ⇒ we feel” or “allostasis math ⇒ consciousness.”

## 2) Measured vs inferred

| Kind | Examples | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|------|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Measured** | Self-report scales; facial coding; HR/GSR/EEG/fMRI correlates; approach/avoid behavior; hormone assays | Correlations, interventions, model fits under a theory | Direct readout of “what it is like” |
| **Inferred** | “Subject is afraid”; “valence is negative”; “shared feeling with observer” | Working hypotheses for prediction/control | Proof of phenomenology in another system (human, animal, or machine) |
| **Named in software** | `valence=-0.3`, `mood=sad`, sentiment API output | Control signals / UI / logs | That the runtime *experiences* anything |

**Rule:** treat first-person feeling reports in humans as data *about reports*; treat agent tokens about “I feel” as **behavior/text**, not as VERIFY of phenomenology.
